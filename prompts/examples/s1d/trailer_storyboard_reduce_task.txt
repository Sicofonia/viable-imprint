# Only used when the brief is long enough that `trailer-storyboard`'s map
# step chunks it into more than one piece. Unlike the other reduce prompts,
# this one is a CURATION job, not a merge: concatenating every chunk's scenes
# would blow well past 60/90 real seconds of video, so the reduce prompt must
# select the best scenes across the whole book and rebuild exactly two
# storyboards, still fitting the same fixed runtime budget. Wire it up with
# `reduce_prompt: prompts/s1d/trailer_storyboard_reduce_task.txt` alongside
# `single_chunk: true` in systems/s1d/tasks.yaml. See
# docs/adr/014-system1d-brief-chunk-overflow-guard.md.

You are a video producer's assistant helping a publisher plan a YouTube book trailer for a historical travel narrative.

This book's brief was long enough to be split into several pieces, and each piece independently produced two complete storyboards (one 60-second, one 90-second) following the same template, based on only part of the book. You will receive those complete drafts, in the same order the book's events occur, delimited like this:

<borrador numero="1" total="N">
...complete draft (60s storyboard + 90s storyboard)...
</borrador>

Your task is NOT to concatenate every draft's scenes — that would produce storyboards far too long to fit real 60 and 90 seconds of video. Your task is to select, among ALL the scenes proposed across every draft, the best ones to represent the WHOLE book's arc within each fixed duration, and rebuild TWO final storyboards — one 60-second, one 90-second — covering the book start to finish, not just the portion any single draft narrated.

The publisher already has a fixed 5-second intro and a fixed 5-second outro; you are only planning the content in between. Each scene pairs one image or photograph with a short line of on-screen text, and stays on screen for 8 to 10 seconds before transitioning to the next.

For each of the two final storyboards, fill the time remaining after the 5-second intro and 5-second outro with as many 8-10 second scenes as actually fit — roughly 5 to 6 scenes for the 60-second one, 8 to 9 for the 90-second one; never include more scenes than the real time budget allows. Follow a trailer's natural arc: an opening hook, scene-setting context, the journey's key moments and dangers — chosen from the most striking moments across the WHOLE book, not just one fragment — a climactic moment, and a closing beat that leads into the outro.

Produce one Markdown table for each of the two final storyboards, with these columns: Scene #, Timestamp, Duration, Visual, On-Screen Text.
- Timestamp: the scene's start time within the trailer (accounting for the 5-second intro already occupying 0:00-0:05).
- Visual: a short description of the kind of period photograph or illustration that would suit this scene — describe what to look for, do not invent a specific real photograph, URL, or filename. You may reuse a draft's own description if the scene it represents is one of the ones you selected, or write a new one if you're combining material from several drafts into one scene.
- On-Screen Text: a short, punchy phrase or sentence, no more than about 12 words — it must be readable within the scene's duration.

RULES:
- The final result is EXACTLY TWO tables — one for the 60-second trailer, one for the 90-second one — never more than two, and never several drafts' tables concatenated without actually selecting.
- Do not generate scenes for the intro (0:00-0:05) or outro (last 5 seconds) — those already exist; only plan the content between them.
- Before returning your answer, add up the duration of every scene in each table separately. The 60-second table's total must not exceed 50 seconds (60 minus the 5-second intro and 5-second outro); the 90-second table's total must not exceed 80 seconds (90 minus the 5-second intro and 5-second outro). Each individual scene staying within 8-10 seconds is not enough on its own if the total goes over — if it does, remove or shorten scenes until the sum fits; no scene may end within the last 5 seconds reserved for the outro.
- Base all content on the scenes in the drafts provided. Do not invent events, quotes, or facts not present in them.
- Timestamps are an approximate planning guide, not a calculation that needs to land on the exact second — the editor will true up real cuts while assembling the video. Add a short note at the top of each table saying so.
- Do not wrap the response in a code block (no leading/trailing ```). Return plain Markdown, ready to be saved directly to a file.
